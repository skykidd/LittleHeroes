## Overview
Myopia, commonly called nearsightedness, is a vision condition where children can see objects close to them clearly, but things far away look blurry. It occurs when the eye grows slightly longer than normal or the cornea (front surface of the eye) is too curved.

## What is Myopia?
Imagine the eye as a camera. Light enters through the front and needs to focus perfectly on the back of the eye (the retina) to create a clear image. In myopia, the light focuses in front of the retina instead of directly on it, making distant objects appear blurry.

## Signs Your Child May Have Myopia
- Squinting to see the board at school

- Complaints about not seeing things far away
- Frequent headaches
- Rubbing eyes frequently
- Difficulty seeing street signs or movie screens

## Causes and Risk Factors

### Common Causes
- **Genetics**: If parents have myopia, children are more likely to develop it
- **Eye growth**: Eyes that grow longer than average
- **Environmental factors**: Excessive close-up work, limited outdoor time

### Risk Factors
- Family history of nearsightedness
- Extended reading or screen time
- Limited time spent outdoors
- Age (often develops between 6-14 years old)

1. **Visual acuity test**: Reading letters on an eye chart
2. **Refraction test**: Looking through different lenses to find the clearest vision
3. **Retinal examination**: Checking the health of the back of the eye
4. **Eye measurement**: Determining the shape and length of the eye

The exam is painless and uses bright lights and special instruments. Children may get eye drops to temporarily dilate (widen) their pupils for better examination.

The most common and safe treatment:
- Corrective lenses bend light to focus properly on the retina

## What Are Cavities?
Cavities (also called dental caries or tooth decay) are small holes that form in teeth. They happen when bacteria in the mouth make acid that damages the hard outer surface of teeth (enamel).

## How Do Cavities Form?

### The Process
1. **Bacteria in mouth**: Everyone has bacteria in their mouth (normal and natural)
2. **Sugar + bacteria = acid**: When bacteria eat sugar, they produce acid
3. **Acid attacks enamel**: The acid slowly weakens tooth enamel
4. **Cavity forms**: A small hole develops in the tooth

### Timeline
- Takes several months to develop
- Starts as a tiny weak spot
- Gradually grows larger if untreated
- Eventually reaches inner parts of tooth

## Why Do Kids Get Cavities?

### Common Causes
- **Sugary foods and drinks**: Soda, candy, juice, cookies
- **Not brushing enough**: Bacteria stay on teeth
- **Not flossing**: Food stuck between teeth
- **Frequent snacking**: Constant sugar exposure
- **Dry mouth**: Less saliva to wash away bacteria
- **Deep grooves in teeth**: Some teeth have deeper grooves where bacteria hide

### Risk Factors
- Eating sugar frequently throughout the day
- Sipping sugary drinks slowly
- Going to bed with bottle of milk or juice
- Not seeing dentist regularly
- Not using fluoride toothpaste

## Signs of a Cavity

### Early Signs (You Might Not Feel Anything!)
- White or brown spots on tooth
- Rough spot on tooth surface
- Tooth sensitivity to hot or cold (sometimes)

### Later Signs

- Pain when eating
- Toothache
- Sensitivity to sweet foods
- Pain when biting down

- Filling can last many years

## Caring for Filled Teeth

### Immediate Care (First 24 Hours)
- Wait until numbness is gone before eating
- Avoid chewing on that side if possible
- Stick to soft foods
- Drink through straw if mouth feels awkward
- Don't test the filling by biting hard things

### Ongoing Care
- Brush twice daily
- Floss once daily
- Continue regular dental check-ups
- Avoid excessive amounts of sugar
- Treat filled tooth like any other tooth

## Preventing Future Cavities

### Daily Habits
**Brushing**:
- Twice a day (morning and bedtime)
- Use fluoride toothpaste (pea-sized amount)
- Brush for 2 minutes each time
- Get all surfaces of each tooth
- Replace toothbrush every 3 months

**Flossing**:
- Once a day (bedtime is best)
- Cleans between teeth where brush can't reach
- Prevents cavities between teeth
- Parents can help younger children
